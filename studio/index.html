02

The Anti-Drift Rule

stop the 7:59 problem

Why tracks drift

Suno v4.5/v5 has a generation budget of ~8 minutes. If your arrangement ends before that, Suno keeps going, drifts into a "different song" (new key/mood), then cuts off hard around 7:59.

Fight it three ways

  • Hard terminal tag after the outro: [End] or [Fade Out].
  • Never write "extended" or "target X min" — use concise single arc | resolve and end | do not extend past the outro.
  • Keep the arrangement lean — less runway to wander.

Fallback

If it still drifts, cut everything after the real outro in the DAW. The drift is past your ending anyway. [End] is a strong nudge, not a hard gate.

Intro / Outro truth

Suno can't count bars — [8-Bar Kick Intro] is a hint, not a measurement. Extend only grows a song forward; there's no "add before the start." Generate one-shot, trim exact in the DAW.

03

DJ Trim Maths

where to cut the kick

One beat = 60 ÷ BPM sec. 32 beats = 1920 ÷ BPM. 64 beats = 3840 ÷ BPM. Cut on a downbeat.

04

Pipe-Tag Conventions

how Suno reads tags

Pipes, not commas

Suno samples from comma lists (reads as noise) but treats pipe-separated elements as directed instructions. Use | inside bracket tags, 3–5 elements max.

[Breakdown | kick drops out | filtered atmosphere | dark pads]

Mix descriptor

One per track, threaded into each section: Dry · Tight Punchy · Warm Analog · Deep Spacious · Wide Reverb · Dubby Delayed · Wall of Sound · Underground Stripped. Drops auto-get a bigger feel (Deep Punchy Groove for deep house).

Exclude box

Hard negation, more reliable than "no X" in the prompt. Common: radio edit, abrupt ending, pop chorus, diva belting. Afro adds steel drum/tropical; deep adds aggressive drop/festival synths.

Vocal density

Terry keeps vocals sparse — short hooks, chops, delay throws, main lyrics in the breakdown, drops mostly instrumental. Dub = 85–90% instrumental, no pop chorus, no full verses.

05

Advanced Settings

Weirdness · Style Influence · Audio Influence

Suno's Custom Mode hides three sliders that quietly shape 20–30% of your output. They aren't quality knobs — higher isn't better — they change how Suno interprets your prompt. For club tracks you almost always want tight style adherence and controlled weirdness.

◆ Weirdness (Safe → Chaos)

How conventional or unexpected the generation can be. 50% is Suno's "normal." Low = predictable and genre-accurate; high = intricate rhythms, odd instruments, surprising transitions.

  • Club tracks: 20–40%. Keeps the groove reliable and mixable.
  • 50% if you want a little character but still listenable.
  • 80%+ = genuine chaos. Above ~81% it can stop resembling music. Only for sound-design/loops, not full tracks.

◆ Style Influence (Loose → Strong)

How literally Suno follows your Style field. Since you've built a precise pipe-divided prompt, you want it followed closely.

  • Club tracks: 75–100%. Your curated prompt is the whole point — make Suno obey it.
  • 40–70% when the lyrics should drive and the style is a loose guide.
  • 0–30% = pure exploration; Suno treats tags as hints and may drift off-genre.

◆ Audio Influence (only with an upload)

Appears only when you upload a reference/melody/vocal. Controls how strongly that audio anchors the new track.

  • High (70–100%) to keep an uploaded melody, rhythm or vocal identity.
  • Low (20–40%) for a loose reference you want reinterpreted.
  • Resolve conflicts first: if the upload and the Style field disagree, pick which one leads — don't max both.

How they interact

High Weirdness + high Style Influence = unusual but still inside your genre. High Weirdness + low Style Influence = full chaos, no guardrails. Move one slider at a time and regenerate — moving all three blind is why outputs "miss for reasons you can't diagnose."

GoalWeirdnessStyle Influence

Starting point if unsure: Weirdness 30%, Style Influence 85% for a tight club track. If it comes out competent but anonymous, raise Weirdness 5–10%. If it drifts off-genre, raise Style Influence. Protect a good result by changing only what's wrong.

The Big Picture

the headline number
#1
EDM was America's fastest-growing genre in the first half of 2026, gaining more of the US listening market than any other genre — per Luminate's 2026 Midyear Report. Festival mainstage proof

Drum & bass breaks out in the US

Higher energy, broadly

Across the board, dancefloors are trending toward higher-energy styles — reflected in DnB's rise, Hard/Industrial Techno holding as SoundCloud's 3rd most-played genre, and hardcore/hardstyle tracks over 180 BPM increasing three years running.

Wider Currents

what's shaping the scene beyond genre

Genre fusion is the safe bet

Across multiple 2026 breakdowns, blending two genres' strengths (e.g. Melodic Techno's harmonic structure over Afro House's drums) keeps coming up as the most reliable path to a track that lands in more than one scene's sets.

AI production tools, mainstream now

AI-assisted production (beat-making, sound design, mastering) is described as a "game-changer" across 2026 industry coverage — not a novelty anymore, standard tooling for producers at every level.

Festival economics under strain

Rising costs and squeezed discretionary income are pressuring festivals that compete purely on headliner names. Events built around a strong specific identity (genre, city, curation) are described as more resilient than generic multi-genre lineups.

Christian EDM (CEDM), an emerging niche

A small but real niche — AXIOM Label Group and others betting that 2026's younger, more religious EDM-listening demographic wants professional-grade dance music that speaks to their values. Early days; not yet close to mainstream.

How to Layer It

quick workflow

1 · Generate the vocal

Copy any prompt, paste into Suno as a new song. Because it's a vocals-forward, sparse prompt over a matching drum feel, you get usable MC stabs with space around them.

2 · Isolate if needed

Use Suno's stem/vocal split (or any stem tool) to pull just the vocal if the prompt gave you a bit of backing. You want the dry-ish MC phrases.

3 · Layer & throw

Drop the vocal under your track in the DAW. Add reverb + delay throws on the tails so stabs ring out. Keep them sparse — breakdown and the first bar of the drop.

4 · Match the key

MC stabs are mostly rhythmic so key rarely clashes, but if a sung phrase sits oddly, nudge its pitch a semitone or two to sit with your track.
